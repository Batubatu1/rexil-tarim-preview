Four benefits you can measure in the field

The four points below describe what silicon does inside the plant. The photographs from the field are on the field evidence page.

  1. 01

    A difference you can see in the field

    A silicon-strengthened plant roots deeper and uses water more efficiently. In the photographs coming back from the field, that shows up as fuller cobs, more tillers and larger fruit.

  2. 02

    Better quality and shelf life

    A reinforced cell wall means firmer flesh and tougher skin. Bruising and moisture loss after harvest drop, so produce reaches the market with less waste.

  3. 03

    Drought and heat tolerance

    The silicon layer in the leaf regulates transpiration and supports stomatal closure. The crop passes through heat waves and water restriction with far less yield loss.

  4. 04

    Lower chemical dependency

    Physically strengthened tissue makes life harder for fungal pathogens and sucking insects. Growers report gradually reducing both spray frequency and fertiliser rates.

The problem is not quantity, it is form

  • Cell wall

    Silicon accumulates in the cell wall and physically reinforces the tissue. Stems stand straighter, lodging drops, fruit is firmer.

  • Stress tolerance

    It supports the plant’s antioxidant defence system, reducing yield loss under drought, heat and salinity.

  • Nutrient uptake

    Root mass and root surface area increase, raising the uptake efficiency of nitrogen, phosphorus and micronutrients.

Silicon makes up roughly a quarter of the earth’s crust and is abundant in soil. Yet plant roots cannot take it up as it is. Root cells can only draw in mono-silicic acid, Si(OH)₄, through dedicated transporter proteins.

Here is the problem: mono-silicic acid is unstable in nature. On contact with water it polymerises quickly, linking into large silica chains the plant can no longer absorb. So even where soil silicon is plentiful, the share the plant can actually use stays low.

The stabilisation technology in Rexil Tarım products holds silicic acid in its mono form both in the container and in the spray tank. The plant can use virtually all of the silicon applied.
